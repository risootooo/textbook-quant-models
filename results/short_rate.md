# Model 5 — Short-rate calibration: Vasicek & CIR on the real 3-month T-bill

Generated by `cases/short_rate_case.py` on 2026-11-08.

## Data

- Source: **U.S. Treasury Daily Par Yield Curve Rates (official TextView pages), 3 Mo tenor**.
- Sample: 2019-01-02 to 2026-11-06, **1964 daily observations** (business days, dt = 1/252).
- Series range: min 0.00%, max 5.63%, last observation **4.19%** (2026-11-06).
- Yields converted from percent to decimals before fitting.
- The published series contains **2 prints of exactly 0.00%** (25–26 March 2020, the COVID dash for cash). For the CIR fit only, those are floored at 0.005% (half the 0.01% print resolution) because the CIR transition density is undefined at r <= 0; Vasicek uses the series as published.

## Result 1 — the full sample cannot identify these models

Fitting 2019–2026 as one regime, the exact MLE runs to the boundary of the parameter space for both models:

| Model | kappa | theta | sigma | log-likelihood |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vasicek | 0.0096 | 26.4719% | 0.5273% | 12,938.2 |
| CIR | 0.0010 | 173.7661% | 5.6628% | 12,560.4 |

kappa at (or near) the 0.001 search bound with an absurd theta is not a typo and not a solver failure — it is the likelihood speaking. The exact Vasicek profile log-likelihood (theta and sigma profiled out in closed form) rises monotonically as kappa shrinks:

| kappa | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.40 | 0.80 | boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| profile log-lik | 12,938.1 | 12,937.7 | 12,936.1 | 12,929.4 | 12,902.9 | 12,938.2 |

Over 2019–2026 the bill rate lived in three different worlds (~2.4% in 2019, ~0.05% at the zero lower bound in 2020–21, ~5.3% after the 2022–23 hiking cycle). A single-regime mean-reverting model reads those level shifts as a near-unit root: the daily AR(1) slope is 0.99996, so the data say 'no detectable reversion to any constant level', and theta — the level it would revert to — is unidentified. Any single pair (kappa, theta) quoted for the full sample would be false precision. What the full sample *can* rank is fit: **Vasicek** wins by 378 log points, because its constant volatility can sit at the ZLB printing 1–5 bp daily moves, while CIR's volatility vanishes as r -> 0 and scores those same moves as enormous shocks.

## Result 2 — headline calibration: the post-ZLB regime

Fitting only the current regime (2022-01-03 to 2026-11-06, 1212 observations — after the ZLB episode ended and the hiking cycle began) gives interior, economically readable estimates:

| Model | kappa | theta | sigma | log-likelihood | half-life |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vasicek | 0.9807 | 5.0031% | 0.5883% | 7,851.5 | 0.71 yrs |
| CIR | 0.4745 | 5.9330% | 3.9365% | 7,564.2 | 1.46 yrs |

- **Half-life of shocks**: ~0.71 years (Vasicek) / ~1.46 years (CIR) — in this regime, bill-rate shocks do measurably decay.
- **Fit**: Vasicek again, by 287 log points on equal parameter counts. (CIR's sigma is not comparable to Vasicek's: CIR volatility at level r is sigma·sqrt(r), e.g. 0.88% at r = 5%.)
- **Feller check (CIR)**: 2·kappa·theta = 0.056306 vs sigma^2 = 0.001550 → condition **HOLDS** (margin +0.054756).
- **Robustness**: refitting on 2023+ only gives Vasicek kappa = 0.187, theta = 4.1809% and CIR kappa = 0.199, theta = 4.2104% — same neighbourhood, slower reversion once the hiking phase itself is excluded. The parameters are regime descriptions, not constants of nature.

## Result 3 — model-implied zero yields vs the actual curve

The post-ZLB fits are fed the last observed short rate (4.19%, 2026-11-06) and asked for zero yields. Actuals are the published par yields for that day.

| Tenor | Vasicek zero | CIR zero | Actual par |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Yr | 4.65% | 4.80% | 4.51% |
| 5 Yr | 4.84% | 5.26% | 4.64% |
| 10 Yr | 4.92% | 5.56% | 4.86% |

The models land in the right neighbourhood at 2y and drift high at 10y, pulled toward their thetas (~5–6%): a one-factor curve is entirely determined by today's short rate plus mean reversion, so it cannot reproduce a market curve shaped by expected cuts and term premia. There is also a conceptual gap that no recalibration fixes: the parameters above are maximum-likelihood under the *physical* measure (how rates actually moved), while bond prices are *risk-neutral* objects. Feeding P-parameters into the pricing formulas — as done here, deliberately — measures the P/Q wedge as much as it measures the models. Par yields are additionally not zero yields, worth a few bp of the gap at most.

## Method in one paragraph

Vasicek's exact Gaussian transition and CIR's exact noncentral chi-square transition (scipy.stats.ncx2) are maximised by bounded L-BFGS-B over log-transformed positive parameters (kappa in [0.001, 50]; bounds exist precisely because of the Result-1 ridge), from AR(1)-moment and generic starts, best optimum kept. Bond prices use the affine closed forms P(tau) = exp(A(tau) - B(tau) r). Simulation (exercised in the test suite) uses the exact transition for Vasicek and full-truncation Euler for CIR, and the suite verifies that both MLEs recover known parameters from simulated data — so the strange full-sample answer above is a property of the data, not of the estimator.

## Limitations (stated, not buried)

- **Weak identification is the headline, not a footnote.** On any sample spanning rate regimes, kappa and theta for these models are barely identified; treat point estimates as regime summaries. Sub-sample sensitivity (2022+ vs 2023+) is reported above rather than averaged away.
- **Single factor.** Both models move the entire curve with one state variable; real curves twist and bend independently (in the sibling rates-lab PCA of the full curve, level explains ~80% of variance — the other ~20% is invisible here by construction).
- **Physical vs risk-neutral.** See Result 3: P-measure calibration plus Q-measure pricing formulas is a diagnostic, not a pricing methodology.
- **Vasicek allows negative rates.** This sample contains the ZLB episode, where that 'flaw' is empirically convenient and CIR's strict positivity is a straightjacket (it is most of why CIR loses the likelihood race here).
- **Constant parameters within each fit.** Even the post-ZLB window contains a hiking cycle and a plateau; the parameters average over both.
- **dt convention.** Daily business-day spacing is treated as exactly 1/252 year; weekends and holidays are ignored, as is standard at this level of the textbook.
- **Data prints.** Two exact 0.00% prints (March 2020) were floored for the CIR fit as documented in the Data section; par yields are used as published, unadjusted.
